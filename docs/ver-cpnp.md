## What this result is, and is not

`PROFILE.md` §5: the corpus establishes a test, and until a second, independently built toolchain
reproduces every digest, "the cross-producer comparability that dropping PROVISIONAL unlocks has an
evaluated population of one." This document is **one more toolchain evaluated** and nothing bigger:

- The toolchain is independently built in the sense §5 means: no Pillow, libjpeg, libwebp, libtiff,
  zlib or littleCMS anywhere; pure Rust, with the JPEG and colour-management arithmetic written in
  this repository. It is *not* independent of the reference's choices: our JPEG and CMS paths were
  written to execute the same published algorithms libjpeg-turbo and littleCMS execute, because the
  profile pins those libraries and a byte-exact digest leaves no other route.
- The evaluation is ours, run on our CI, over the vendored copy of the corpus (byte-identical to the
  release; a test pins the manifest's SHA-256 `d743f077…09b5e`). Nobody else has re-run it.
- Conformance for `verd-pillow/1` covers what corpus release 1 pins (§6 of the profile lists what it
  does not: non-sRGB ICC, the fail-closed rule, deep sources, HDR, AVIF/HEIC, progressive /
  arithmetic / CMYK JPEG, animated WebP and APNG, large images). Where this document reports
  agreement beyond the corpus, it is our own measurement against the pinned Pillow build, not a
  conformance claim.
- A Record carries no profile identifier (VER 1.0.x and the 1.1 draft have no field for one). This
  page is where LightCraft states its profile, outside the Record, as `PROFILE.md` §4 asks.

## The JPEG decoders already in the workspace

`zune-jpeg` and `jpeg-decoder` were tried first, as the task required; both are stb_image-derived
and neither reproduces libjpeg's islow IDCT, so both miss **every** JPEG entry, the 4:4:4 one
(no upsampling involved) included. Measured against our decoder, whose output reproduces the corpus
digests and is therefore libjpeg-turbo's (from `tests/integration/decoders.rs`):

| Asset | Decoder | Samples that differ | Max Δ | Histogram (Δ: count) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `jpeg-q92-444.jpg` | zune-jpeg 0.5.15 | 589 of 36 864 | 2 | 1: 500, 2: 89 |
| `jpeg-q92-444.jpg` | jpeg-decoder 0.3.2 | 1 536 of 36 864 | 3 | 1: 1 275, 2: 257, 3: 4 |
| `jpeg-q75-420.jpg` | zune-jpeg 0.5.15 | 9 076 of 36 864 | 3 | 1: 5 594, 2: 3 465, 3: 17 |
| `jpeg-q75-420.jpg` | jpeg-decoder 0.3.2 | 2 775 of 36 864 | 3 | 1: 1 964, 2: 806, 3: 5 |
| `jpeg-orientation-1.jpg` | zune-jpeg 0.5.15 | 257 of 18 432 | 3 | 1: 209, 2: 47, 3: 1 |
| `jpeg-orientation-1.jpg` | jpeg-decoder 0.3.2 | 808 of 18 432 | 3 | 1: 662, 2: 143, 3: 3 |

`jpeg-decoder` does implement triangle upsampling, but its `h2v2` rounds both output phases with
`+8` where libjpeg-turbo uses `+8` / `+7`, and its colour conversion is not `jdcolor.c`'s. Hence a
decoder of our own: the islow IDCT, the three fancy upsamplers and the colour tables are small,
fully specified integer algorithms, and matching them is the whole of what the profile asks of a
JPEG path. LightCraft's photo pipeline keeps using `lightcraft-codecs`' decoders; only CPNP-1 uses
this one.

## The ICC entry, and what `moxcms` does to sRGB

`png-srgb-icc.png` embeds littleCMS's own built-in sRGB profile ("sRGB built-in", parametric type-3
curves, s15Fixed16 colorants). The profile measured lcms's sRGB → sRGB transform as exact identity
on 8-bit input, so the entry shares its digest with the untagged PNG.

`moxcms` (the CMS `lightcraft-codecs` uses) **perturbs that transform**. Over the full 2²⁴ RGB cube
through that exact profile:

| `moxcms` 0.9.1 path | Colours moved | By 1 | By 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8-bit transform, `prefer_fixed_point` (default) | 15 351 466 (91.5 %) | 15 282 346 | 69 120 |
| 8-bit transform, floating point | 15 985 451 (95.3 %) | 15 937 486 | 47 965 |
| `f32` transform, rounded once by us | 347 807 (2.1 %) | 347 807 | 0 |
| 16-bit transform, lcms 16→8 rounding | 350 193 (2.1 %) | | |

The 8-bit path truncates (the grey axis moves too: 1 → 0, 2 → 1, 5 → 4, …), and its tone curves
are table-interpolated. So matrix/TRC profiles do not go through `moxcms`: `src/cms.rs` evaluates
them as littleCMS 2.19 evaluates an 8-bit RGB → RGB matrix/TRC transform. When the joined matrix is
the identity to within 1/65535 the matrices drop out and lcms joins the two curves into a 4096-point
table, which is the identity when every entry is within 0x0f of linear (that is this entry). Otherwise
`OptimizeMatrixShaper`: 256-entry input shapers in 1.14 fixed point, the joined matrix in 1.14 fixed
point, a 16385-entry output shaper rounded to 8 bits. A test runs the whole 2²⁴ cube through the
corpus's profile and requires identity.

Black point compensation: for a matrix/TRC source whose curves map 0 to 0 both black points are zero
and lcms's BPC is the identity, which is why this path needs none. A source curve with a non-zero
floor (parametric types 2 and 4 with an offset) is the one matrix/TRC case where lcms's BPC would
move black and this path does not. LUT-based, CMYK and Lab profiles go through `moxcms`'s `f32`
transform, which has **no BPC at all** and is not lcms-exact: no corpus entry exercises them
(`PROFILE.md` §6) and we claim nothing for them.

## Where the profile leaves room, and what we chose

Release 1 pins 21 behaviours. Elsewhere the reference implementation and a normative errata item
sometimes disagree; where they do, **the errata item wins** here, and the case is listed. None of
these cases has a corpus digest.

| Case | Reference (`verd` + Pillow 12.3.0) | LightCraft | Why | Measured difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16-bit RGB/RGBA/LA PNG and TIFF | Pillow keeps the high byte (`v >> 8`) | `round_half_up(v·255/65535)` | E3: the quantise step is a scale | a 40×30 RGB16 test image: 920 of 3 600 samples differ, all by 1 |
| 16-bit gray PNG (`I;16`) | Pillow clips (`min(v, 255)`) | scale, as above | E3 (and `PROFILE.md` §6 calls the clip out) | 3 591 of 3 600 samples differ, by up to 254 |
| XMP `tiff:Orientation` without EXIF tag 274 | Pillow's `getexif()` falls back to the XMP value | ignored: upright | E6: IFD0 tag 274 only | rotation, not a pixel delta |
| Tag 274 stored as LONG (or count ≠ 1) | Pillow accepts it | upright | E6: "a tag whose type is not SHORT" is 1 | rotation |
| GIF frame 0 a sub-rectangle with **no** transparency index | uncovered area is palette index 0 | transparent → white; `alpha_present` true | E7 (`PROFILE.md` §3.2 and §6 record this as the reference's divergence) | the uncovered area's colour |
| GIF frame 0 larger than the logical screen | Pillow grows the canvas | the same | mirrors the reference; E7 does not address overrun | none |
| A GIF `ICCRGBG1` profile | Pillow does not read it | not consumed (`icc_was_present` false) | mirrors the reference | none |
| PNG `iCCP` whose zlib stream is damaged | Pillow's `zlib.error` path reads it as *absent* (assume sRGB) | `UnusableIcc`, no buffer | E8: present-but-unusable fails closed | no buffer vs one |
| Gray + alpha with a gray profile | Pillow converts to RGB first, then lcms refuses the gray profile: no buffer | the same (`UnusableIcc`) | mirrors the reference | none |
| Animated WebP whose `VP8X` has no alpha flag and whose frame 0 does not cover the canvas | libwebp's RGBA canvas read as RGBX: uncovered area black | `image-webp` 0.2.4 gives RGB: uncovered area black | E7 would make it white; left as the reference does it, a known gap | the uncovered area |
| TIFF with unspecified `ExtraSamples` | Pillow `RGBX`: the fourth sample is dropped | the same | mirrors the reference | none |
| TIFF associated alpha | Pillow `RGBa` → `RGBA` (truncating un-premultiply) | the same | mirrors the reference | none |
| HDR gain map | byte scan for three markers | the same | §3.8 | none |
| AVIF, HEIC | decoded (AVIF) by libavif | `Unsupported` | no pure-Rust AV1 decoder in this build | no buffer |

## Perceptual digests

`pdq/1.0` and `phash-dct/64` are not part of the decoder profile (`PROFILE.md` §3.9; E22 defers
their vectors to the 1.1 registry), but E22 names their reference forms, and LightCraft computes
exactly those (`lightcraft_vera::digest`, used by `lightcraft_ver::hashing`), over the **full**
Canonical Buffer, never a thumbnail:

- **`pdq/1.0`** is Meta's PDQ as ThreatExchange publishes it (`threatexchange` 1.2.16's
  `pdq_hasher`, which wraps the C++ reference through `pdqhash` 0.2.8; there is no separate
  pure-Python PDQ in it, so the oracle *is* the C++ reference). Ported step for step in `f32`: the
  luma `pdqhash` forms (`0.299·R + 0.587·G + 0.114·B` in double, stored as `f32`), two rounds of
  the Jarosz box filter (rows then columns, window `⌈dimension / 128⌉`, the reference's four-phase
  running sum, including for a window of 1, where the running add-and-subtract still rounds), the
  64×64 point decimation (skipped for a 64×64 input, as the reference does), the image-domain
  quality, the 16×64 DCT with the reference's `f32`-rounded scale factor and index-order
  accumulation, Torben's median, and the reference's hex order (word 15 first). Under 5 pixels on
  a side the digest is all zeros with quality 0, as in the reference.
- **`phash-dct/64`** is `imagehash.phash` (4.3.2): PIL's `convert("L")` (16.16 fixed point), PIL's
  Lanczos `resize((32, 32))` (`ImagingResample`: per-output coefficient tables, 22-bit fixed
  point, a horizontal pass over the rows the vertical pass reads, then the vertical pass, each
  rounded to 8 bits), scipy's unnormalised DCT-II along columns then rows, the 8×8 block
  **including DC** against numpy's median, bits row-major. The DCT is evaluated by Lee's even/odd
  recursion rather than a direct cosine sum: like the FFT scipy uses, it produces exact zeros
  where the picture's symmetry makes coefficients vanish, and those zeros decide bits whenever
  they reach the median (`gif-animated-2frame.gif`, whose columns are constant, is such a case).

**Agreement with the oracles: 31 of 31 exact for pHash and 31 of 31 exact for PDQ** (the 21
decoder-corpus Canonical Buffers, fed to the oracles as lossless PNGs of our own buffers, and the
ten reference-set images), PDQ's quality figure included, plus five synthetic patterns (1200×900,
3001×1999, 640×64, 64×64, 5×7) that exercise wide Jarosz windows, large Lanczos supports, the
64×64 shortcut and the minimum size. The vectors, PIL's 32×32 resample of each, and the script that
generated them are `crates/vera/tests/perceptual-vectors.json` and
`crates/vera/tests/gen_perceptual_vectors.py`.

Two points of the references that are easy to get wrong, found by the vectors: the reference PDQ
box filter is not the identity for a window of 1 (its running sum drifts by float rounding, and on a
flat-structured picture that drift is the whole DCT), and the reference pHash of any flat frame is
`8000000000000000` (only DC above the median). The second is why `lightcraft_vera::thresholds`
now gates pHash matches on a quality of at least 1 (`PHASH_MIN_QUALITY`).

## Records and validation (for completeness)

`lightcraft_ver::record::Builder` emits VER 1.0.1 and 1.1-draft Records and refuses any document
with an Error from structural or profile validation. Structural validation is the `jsonschema`
crate (2020-12) against the vendored schemas, asserting `format` and `contentEncoding` as E23
requires; the profile rules carry the upstream validator's codes (`validate::PROFILE_CODES`). Against
the upstream Python validator on the vendored conformance corpus (92 fixtures that are JSON and
present), our findings restricted to the codes we implement are **identical** to the oracle's on
every fixture (61 codes reported by both), and Records built by the test suite are `conformant` to
the oracle under both schemas (`cargo xtask ver oracle`).
